Set up this iPad

Every iPad at a race sends its results to the same place on the results site. Give the race the same name on each iPad, or set up the first one and choose that race from the list on the others. Give each iPad its own place name: that is how their results are told apart.

  • Stand the iPad where it sees runners from the front as they finish, as square-on as you can, and fix it so that it cannot move.
  • Where runners cross the line, the numbers on their bibs should be at least about a fortieth of the height of the picture. Move closer or zoom in if they are smaller.
  • Keep the iPad on power and out of direct sun, with this page open and in front.
  • Open this page on Wi-Fi the first time: it fetches about 15 MB once and keeps it on the iPad.
Recording

Drag the round ends to lay the line where runners finish; the arrow shows the way they run. Drag the square corners to mark where bibs can be: a smaller area is searched faster.

    For fine-tuning

    Left to itself, an iPad reads with one core: reading with several needs something that Safari has been known to refuse on some versions of iPadOS. It is worth trying 2 (or 4 on an iPad with an M-series chip) and keeping whichever shows the most looks a second at the top of the picture. If reading with several cores does not work, the page goes back to one by itself.

    The camera delay is how long a picture takes to get from the lens to the page; it is taken off every time, where the browser does not report it itself.
